Proposed operating model for the Harvard International Office · Associate Director of Operations & Administration · Requisition 004436SR

Accurate records, current systems and a team that stays ahead of the calendar

HIO is the link between Harvard’s international students and scholars and the federal agencies that govern their status. This role keeps the records, the system, the billing and the operations team behind that work accurate and on time. Below is how I would run it, with seven sample reports built on synthetic data sized to HIO.

What I would do first

  1. Protect the federal calendar. Map every registration, reporting and batch deadline for the year, with an owner and a check before each one.
  2. Make the School data clean on arrival. One crosswalk from each School’s Slate or SIS codes to SEVIS, so records match before any I-20 or DS-2019 is drafted.
  3. Give the Director one view. A weekly leadership dashboard and a release gate for every ISSM upgrade, so decisions come with numbers.

Mission and values in the daily work

HIO supports Harvard’s teaching and research mission by serving international students and scholars and acting as their liaison with U.S. government agencies. Harvard’s five community values set how that work is done.

Respect the rights, differences and dignity of othersEvery record is a person’s status. Notices are plain, early and kind, and School contacts are treated as partners.
Honesty and integrity in all dealingsNumbers reconcile before they are reported, and exceptions are shown, never smoothed over.
Pursue excellence conscientiouslyTest before every release, automate the repeatable work and keep people on the judgment calls.
Be accountable for actions and conductEvery exception, defect and invoice has one owner and one date.
Cultivate bonds and bridgesShared definitions with the Schools, HUIT, Campus Services Finance and the hospitals, so one record means the same thing everywhere.

Country data — what the OIRA PDF actually supports

The OIRA country table publishes country × School, not a unique country total. That changes what can honestly be compared or forecast.

A denominator problem, not a rounding error.

Summing the School columns gives 6,836 students, but the University’s unique nonresident total is 6,749. The 87 difference is students registered in more than one School, counted once per School column. The country rows are therefore school-column sums, not unique country headcounts.

Scope & assumptions

What this work sample covers

This is an operating-model work sample, not a career profile. It shows how the work would run, what it produces and what it assumes. Qualifications and experience belong in a separate career profile.

Scope boundary.

This role covers the operating system behind every HIO service — records, systems, data, billing, reporting, team, security and modernization. It does not replace advisors for visa status, employment authorization, academic training or case-specific advice. It serves as DSO/ARO for systems and project management, including travel signatures, and designs the controls that keep every area compliant.

Role assumptionReports to the HIO Director or the Senior Director, International Office, depending on the final reporting line; DSO/ARO designation follows SEVP approval and Director sign-off.
Access assumedRead-only ISSM/ISD, School data, the service log, and working relationships with HUIT and Campus Services Finance.
Team assumed3–5 operations staff, with named backups for batches, billing and School data.
Evidence standardAll figures are synthetic unless cited; no HIO internal data; no case-specific legal advice.

Two judgment calls the routine does not settle

Most of the work follows a set cycle. These two sample situations, from the sample reports, show how I decide when the rules leave room.

Release go or no-go · February 2027

The vendor says the defect is rare. Do we release anyway?
What the rules leave open
The gate requires critical defects fixed or accepted in writing. Accepting it would deliver the other fixes the team asked for on time.
What I weighed
Extensions fail only in batch, but spring is when extensions peak, and a failed batch means a person’s record does not update before the deadline.
The call
No-go. Stay on the current version, patch on February 25, retest on the 26th. The Director gets the decision with the trade-off in one paragraph.

Hospital dispute · $6,200

A hospital disputes a full-year charge for a scholar who left two months early.
What the rules leave open
The agreement is silent on partial terms. Issuance work was done in full, but the record was closed two months early, so no maintenance work was done for those months.
What I weighed
Hold the full $6,200, credit the two unused months, or waive more. FY26 practice with two other hospitals was to bill by month, and the invoice is already past 90 days.
The solution
Bill $5,500 and credit $700. Issuance stays at $2,000, and maintenance is billed for the 10 months the record was open. Payment is due in 30 days, and a partial-term clause goes into every hospital agreement at renewal.
What the Director approves
Two yes or no items: the $700 credit, which is above the proposed $500 credit limit, and the clause for the renewal template.
